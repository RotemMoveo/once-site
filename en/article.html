HaRaz 13 receives its Form 4: the first families move in
By the ONCE content team
After three years of construction, the 18-apartment boutique building on HaRaz Street has received its long-awaited occupancy permit. We toured the apartments with the first families and heard from them why they chose this building in particular.
When we arrived on HaRaz Street early in the morning, the first moving trucks were already parked by the entrance. The Levy family, who bought a four-room apartment on the third floor back at the planning stage, were unloading boxes with visible excitement. “We followed the construction every single week,” says Michal Levy. “Seeing it actually happen — that’s a moment that is hard to describe.”
The building, designed by the Avner Yashar architecture firm, pairs a light-toned façade with generous balconies and a shared roof garden. Every apartment was planned around natural light, flow between spaces and a first-rate technical specification.
A milestone for the new community
Form 4 is far more than a bureaucratic document — it is the moment a building becomes a home. For ONCE this is the third milestone of the year: after successful move-ins at Gluskin 23 and Eisenberg 38, HaRaz 13 joins the company’s family of occupied buildings in Rehovot.
"We built HaRaz 13 the way you build a home for your own family. No compromises, down to the last detail"
Founder & CEO, ONCE
Over the coming weeks the surrounding development works will be completed: paving the pavement, planting shade trees and installing new street lighting. In parallel, the company’s customer service team accompanies every family through the move-in — from handing over the key to a full walkthrough of the smart-home systems.
The shared roof garden. Photo: ONCE Studio
What’s next?
Sales at the company’s next project, KKL 7–9 in the Neve Yehuda neighbourhood, are already in full swing. Anyone looking to join the ONCE community is welcome to book a meeting at the sales office — or simply drop in for a coffee and see up close what building to a different standard looks like.
